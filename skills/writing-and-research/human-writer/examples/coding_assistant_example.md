## After: Direct Human-Writer Output (Clean Prose, No Scaffolding)

AI coding assistants can make you faster at the boring parts. Not everything. Definitely not architecture.

They're great at boilerplate: config files, test scaffolding, repetitive migrations, and simple CRUD endpoints. But they're equally great at sounding confident while hallucinating deprecated library calls. I've accepted suggestions that compiled cleanly, passed every lint rule, and still completely missed the business logic because I wasn't paying attention.

In practice, developers tend to split into two camps. Some treat it like an aggressive autocomplete for chores and review every single line with extreme skepticism. Others disable it entirely after it suggests a subtly broken SQL query for the third time in an afternoon. Both reactions make total sense.

The productivity claims from vendors are notoriously slippery. A 30% suggestion acceptance rate doesn't mean your team ships 30% faster—acceptance isn't correctness, and raw code volume isn't value. Without solid automated tests, you're essentially flying blind.\n
